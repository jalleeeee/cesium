What You're Looking At

This cross-section model shows the three documented mines on the Allee property — Dubois, Indiana, and Lavender — drawn to scale based on ISGS mine inventory data sheets (July 7, 1988).

The gold shafts show where early 1900s miners actually dug. They were mining fluorspar (calcium fluoride) — a valuable industrial mineral, but they had no way to test for rare earth elements.

The red line marks the deepest anyone ever went: 100 feet at the Indiana Mine.

The green zone below represents the untested REE target — the depth range where rare earth element deposits are expected based on the 2024 Terra Nova research by Trela, Freiburg, and Gazel. Their work showed that REE-bearing brine-melts traveled upward from a deep carbonatite source through brecciated fault pathways — including the Illinois Furnace Fault that crosses directly through this property.

The blue plane represents the Illinois Furnace Fault Zone itself — 850 feet of vertical displacement, cutting through the entire property at N60-65°E.

Bottom line: they mined the top 12%. The other 88% — where the REEs should be — has never been touched.
