1. The Geological System

This property sits on the same fault that carried REEs to a world-class deposit

In 2024, Trela, Freiburg, Gazel et al. published a landmark paper in Terra Nova (doi:10.1111/ter.12712) that fundamentally changed our understanding of how rare earth elements were deposited in the Illinois-Kentucky Fluorspar District.

Their key finding: Heavy REE enrichment at Hicks Dome was derived from brine-melts exsolved from a deep carbonatite source. These REE-bearing fluids traveled upward along brecciated fault pathways throughout the district.

The Illinois Furnace Fault Zone — which crosses directly through the Allee property — is one of these brecciated structural conduits. With 850 feet of documented vertical displacement, it represents a major fluid pathway connecting the deep carbonatite source to near-surface deposits.

The Allee property sits at 0 miles on this fault system. Hicks Dome — where ISGS estimates 12–65 million raw tons of critical minerals ore — sits at 7.8 miles along the same system. The Sparks Hill Diatreme, with confirmed REE surface concentrations, sits at 3.1 miles.

The property has never been tested for rare earth elements.
